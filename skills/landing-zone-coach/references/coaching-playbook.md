# Coaching playbook

How to coach someone, one-on-one or in a workshop, to a Landing Zone that is clear, common and coherent.

## Coaching questions by step

### 1. Frame
- What exactly are we defining success for: a product, release, MVP, project, model, or a personal decision?
- Who has a stake in the outcome? Who could declare it a failure?
- Over what period? (First release? First year in market?)
- "Write down your definition of success right now. Would each stakeholder write the same thing?"
- Is this incremental (we know what drove success last time) or novel (we'll need touch-and-go runs)?

### 2. Brainstorm
- What would make *[each stakeholder]* call this a failure?
- What made the last similar effort succeed or fail?
- Walk the attribute catalog: anything in time, quality, performance, business, usage, technology we've missed?
- What do competitors or customers measure us on?

### 3. Cut to the vital few
- "If only this row were missed and everything else was met, would we still call it a failure?" If not, cut it.
- Is this success or scope? (Features belong in the backlog unless their absence means failure.)
- Can these related rows collapse into one summary row plus a child Landing Zone?
- Does this row need upper-management attention regularly? If not, it may belong in a child Landing Zone.

### 4. Quantify and set levels
- How would we measure this? Who measures it, and how often?
- Which direction is better: more or less, sooner or later?
- **Minimum first:** "What's the level where, below it, we've failed?" Then: "What do we plan to hit?" Then: "What's the best realistic outcome, beyond which more effort wouldn't pay back?"
- What evidence supports these numbers? Past products, benchmarks, customer data, a forecast?
- Is this Minimum really a Target in disguise? (Teams often set Minimum at what they *want*, not at what avoids failure.)

### 5. Review and maintain
- Who needs to see this before it's agreed? In what order?
- Where do you expect disagreement? That's useful; plan to surface it.
- Which optional columns help: Now, Kill Switch, Owner?
- When will you revisit it? What events trigger an update (new stakeholder, competitor move, learning from an MVP)?
- Where will it live so people actually see it?

## Common mistakes and how to respond

| Mistake | What it looks like | Coaching response |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope in disguise** | 40+ rows, mostly features | "A Landing Zone captures success, not scope. Which of these, if missing, would make this a failure?" Move features to the backlog or a Functions & constraints list. |
| **Adjectives** | "Fast," "intuitive," "best-in-class" | "Each stakeholder will imagine a different 'fast.' What number would make everyone agree?" |
| **Minimum = Target** | All three columns nearly equal, or Minimum is aspirational | "If you hit this Minimum, would you really call it a failure to miss it by a little? Where's the actual failure line?" |
| **Wrong direction** | Minimum cost lower than Target cost | "For cost, lower is better, so Minimum is the *highest* cost you can accept." |
| **No cap** | Outstanding is "as high as possible" | "Outstanding caps over-processing. Past what point would extra effort stop paying back?" |
| **Ranking rows** | "Row 1 is the most important" | "Every row is required, so importance is equal. Priority changes week to week, so keep it in the backlog." |
| **Built alone** | One person's view, no stakeholder review | "It isn't *common* until stakeholders have seen it. Who should challenge it first?" |
| **Set and forget** | Written at kickoff, never updated | "Success changes as you learn. What will trigger an update?" |
| **Treated as guarantee** | "We hit every row, so it'll sell" | "It's your best current knowledge. Markets can still surprise you, so keep testing assumptions." |
| **Confused with OKRs** | Rows are quarterly team objectives | "OKRs track objectives for a team over time. This defines what makes *this product* succeed." |

## Review rubric

Use this when reviewing a draft. Lead with the three highest-impact fixes.

| Check | Pass when… |
|---|---|
| **Clear** | Every row has a measurable definition and unit; no adjectives standing in for numbers |
| **Common** | Stakeholders who could declare failure have reviewed it, or a plan exists for that |
| **Coherent** | Rows don't contradict each other; levels are achievable *together* (e.g., Outstanding cost and Outstanding date aren't mutually impossible without a stated tradeoff) |
| **Vital few** | ≤ 24 rows, ideally ~12; every row passes the "would missing it alone be failure?" test |
| **Success, not scope** | Features appear only as a Functions & constraints list row |
| **Three levels** | Minimum, Target, Outstanding all filled in, ordered correctly for each row's direction |
| **Minimum is real** | Minimum marks the actual failure line, not an aspiration |
| **Outstanding is a cap** | Beyond it, more effort would not pay back |
| **Measurable now** | There's a known way to measure each row, or a plan to create one |
| **Living** | Owner and review cadence are identified |

## Facilitating a team session (≈90 minutes)

For someone who will coach a team, the workshop flow Dan has taught at Product Camp Dallas and Product League:

1. **Hook (10 min).** Ask everyone to write down the definition of success for the current work, privately. Compare. The differences make the case for the method.
2. **Teach (20 min).** Aircraft-carrier metaphor; anatomy (rows, Minimum / Target / Outstanding); clear, common, coherent; the product-launch and car examples; success vs. scope.
3. **Practice (20 min).** Small groups draft a Landing Zone for a product they can all see (a consumer gadget or app), or a personal one (car, job, home). Low stakes, fast feedback.
4. **Use (15 min).** Decision rights, importance vs. priority, variants (Now, Kill Switch, Owner).
5. **Apply (20 min).** Each team starts a Landing Zone for its real work. Expect it to reveal what nobody knows yet; that's progress.
6. **Close (5 min).** Agree on next steps: who validates it with which stakeholders, and when it'll be reviewed.

**Facilitation tips**
- Get every voice into the brainstorm before anyone starts cutting. Silent writing on sticky notes works well.
- When stakeholders disagree on a level, record both numbers and the reasoning. Resolve with evidence, or escalate to whoever owns the outcome.
- Keep the table visible and on one page throughout.
- If the room can't agree on what success is, the session has succeeded at surfacing the real problem. Name it and plan the follow-up.
